# Consumer research: Netflix & Swiggy

**Two independent survey-based studies from my BBA in Marketing (Amity Global Business School, Bangalore), re-analysed in 2026 from the raw exports with corrected figures, an NPS calculation and hand-coded open-text answers.**

[![Full write-up](https://img.shields.io/badge/Read-Full%20write--up-111?style=for-the-badge)](https://ameer29.github.io/bba-research.html)

| | Netflix (Year 2) | Swiggy (final-year dissertation) |
|---|---|---|
| Question | What drives preference for Netflix, and who would people switch to? | How does Swiggy's business model work, and do customers choose it for the reasons it competes on? |
| Method | 17-question Google Forms survey | Business Model Canvas + 20-question survey |
| Sample | 92 responses (convenience sample, mostly aged 15–30) | 93 responses (same profile) |
| Guide | Prof. Shanti J | Prof. S. Karthikeyan |

## Netflix: key findings
- **81%** satisfied overall. **Content breadth (80%)** and **quality (76%)** rated highest; **pricing lowest (27%)**.
- **Amazon Prime** was the main fallback (56%); **38%** said they'd always choose Netflix.
- **92%** browse the recommendations at least sometimes before choosing what to watch.

## Swiggy: key findings
- **NPS: +48** (54 promoters, 25 passives, 11 detractors; n = 90). *Added in 2026.*
- **70%** say app discounts shape their ordering most, and **84%** name food quality as their top concern…
- …but in open-text answers explaining *why* they pick an app, **food quality came last**. Speed and delivery tracking (18 mentions) and discounts and price (17) led. *Coded in 2026.*

> **Insight:** quality is the baseline every app must meet; it isn't what wins the customer. Customer service was the one reason cited almost only in Swiggy's favour.

## Revisited in 2026: what changed
- Recalculated every figure from the raw exports. Several original figures were off; for example, Netflix's gender split was reported with the labels swapped.
- Added the NPS calculation and coded 90 open-text answers into six themes.
- Limitations I'd now state up front: a student-heavy convenience sample, a few leading questions, and descriptive statistics only (no segmentation or significance tests).
